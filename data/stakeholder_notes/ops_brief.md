# Ops brief: conversion reporting requirements

From: Sales Operations Director, International
To: BI Manager
Re: What my side of the report needs to mean

Thanks for chasing this. My teams live and die by conversion, and the
number has to reflect what actually happened on the phones, not what
arrived in the CRM. A few things I need built in, not bolted on afterwards.

**Conversion.** For my reporting, conversion is policies divided by
connected calls, not by whatever lead count marketing is using. My centres
are resourced and rostered against calls that actually connect - a lead
nobody could reach was never a sales opportunity in the way my agents are
measured, and I'm not having my conversion rate diluted by volume my teams
never had a chance to work.

**Population.** Exclude duplicate leads, and exclude leads created outside
regional business hours. Duplicates inflate call volume without adding
real opportunities, and anything that lands overnight or on a weekend
wasn't a live opportunity until the centre opened - counting it against the
same-day numbers misrepresents what my agents could have done with it.

**Attribution.** Attribute the sale to the agent on the last call before
the application date. That's the agent who did the work that got the
application over the line, whatever happened earlier in the lead's life.
Commission and coaching both run off this.

**Reporting period.** The reporting month is the month the policy started,
not the month the lead first appeared. My forecasting and my team's targets
are built around when business actually completes, and that's the number
finance reconciles against too.

I know Marketing will have their own view of some of this - that's fine,
we run different parts of the funnel - but for anything that reports my
teams' performance, these four points are the definition. Let me know if
you need the SLA thresholds behind "business hours" for each region; Ops
Resource Planning holds those.
